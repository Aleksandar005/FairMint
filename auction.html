Sealed-bid auction over class groups

Each bid is locked in a timelock envelope. Nobody can read it until someone performs T sequential squarings in a group whose order no one knows. The contract is the only source of truth: it checks every opening, decrypts the amounts itself, and picks the winner itself.

Setup, done once, trustless
The fresh randomness (a block hash) seeds the generator, and h = g^(2^T) is computed once and stored in the contract. T is chosen so that unlocking takes longer than the auction itself. That is why unlocking can start the moment a bid arrives, yet no envelope opens before closing time: the work is spread across the auction, and T keeps things secret.

Envelopes on the chain

Unlocking starts automatically as each bid arrives. Envelopes fill up during the auction, but none of them opens before closing time, because T is longer than the auction. The verdict runs on its own once the timer ends.

Bidding is closed. The auctioneer machine is opening the envelopes. The amounts below come from the chain as soon as the contract verifies and decrypts them.

Opening is permissionless by design. The contract will not take anyone's word for an amount, not even the bidder's own: it accepts only w = u^(2^T) with a valid proof, and producing those takes the actual T squarings. Knowing your own bid does not shortcut that work. So if the auctioneer machine disappears, any participant can do the squarings for every envelope right here and the contract still verifies, decrypts and picks the winner. Nobody depends on anyone.

All the cryptography on this page is real and runs in your browser. Envelopes, openings and the verdict live only inside the SealedAuction.sol contract. Opening and finalizing are permissionless, so the auction finishes even if the auctioneer goes offline.
